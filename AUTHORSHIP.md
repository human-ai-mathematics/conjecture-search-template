# Authorship and credit

**Who signs what comes out of a search, how a contribution is credited before any paper
exists, and how priority within the record is established.**

A mathematician who takes part in a public search needs to know this before they start.
This file is the default policy of a program instantiated from the template. A program may
adapt it, and says so in its brief; it should not leave the question unanswered.

## Who signs a paper

Everyone who made a **substantive contribution** to the results a paper reports co-signs it,
in alphabetical order, as is usual in mathematics. A contribution is substantive when, without
it, a result in the paper would be missing or would not be trusted. It includes:

- posing a statement the paper proves or refutes, or the reformulation that made it tractable;
- proposing the approach, reduction or example that a proof or counterexample rests on;
- writing a proof or a refutation, or finding a counterexample;
- certifying a result, by an independent review or a human acceptance;
- writing a substantial part of the manuscript.

Reporting a typo, asking a question or pointing to a known reference is acknowledged in the
text, not on the byline. Anyone may decline to sign.

**AI systems are not authors.** They cannot take responsibility for a paper, and arXiv and
most journals do not accept them as authors
([arXiv policy](https://blog.arxiv.org/2023/01/31/arxiv-announces-new-policy-on-chatgpt-and-similar-tools)).
The agents' share of the work is stated in the paper, with the models used, and the record
shows it in detail.

An idea proposed by a person and turned into a proof by an agent is that person's
contribution: the record links the idea to the result it led to.

## Who decides

The program's maintainers, named in the brief, propose the author list before submission, in
an issue open to everyone who contributed. A disagreement is settled there before the paper is
submitted. The record (below) is the evidence each side points to.

## Credit before a paper

The repository is citable on its own. Its `CITATION.cff` lists the people who contributed to
the search, and each release archived on [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/) gets a DOI, so that a
contribution can be cited, and named on a CV, before any paper exists.

## Priority within the record

The public Git history and the dated checkpoints timestamp every contribution, including an
idea proposed in an issue, which the node it led to links back to. This does not stop someone
from racing to a paper with what they read here, but it makes priority within the record
unambiguous and taking an idea without credit visible.
